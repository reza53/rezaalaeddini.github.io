Short tandem repeats (STRs) are specific regions of DNA where short sequences of 2 to 6 base pairs are repeated directly in a row, with the number of these repeated units varying greatly between individuals, making them highly useful for forensic analysis ([ANDE STR Analysis Explained](https://www.ande.com/str-analysis-explained){:target="_blank"}).

### Technical Challenges in Forensic STR Profiling

While short tandem repeat (STR) multiplex systems offer robust identification capabilities, forensic practitioners regularly encounter significant biological complications during sample analysis. Low-template DNA profiles frequently exhibit stochastic effects, such as locus drop-out, where one or both alleles fail to amplify, or drop-in, where trace environmental contaminants introduce foreign alleles.

Furthermore, interpreting complex DNA mixtures—samples containing genetic material from three or more contributors—remains a major hurdle. Differential amplification can cause severe peak height imbalance, complicating the distinction between true alleles and PCR artifacts like stutter products. When biological samples undergo environmental degradation, preferential amplification of smaller fragments occurs, resulting in severe locus drop-out across larger molecular weight markers and requiring advanced probabilistic genotyping software for reliable interpretation.
