Hair dye is significant in forensic science because it provides uniquely identifiable trace evidence that narrows down suspect profiles. 
Since treated or dyed hair is structurally altered, it is often easier to differentiate and individualize than untreated hair, allowing examiners to connect a strand at a crime scene to a specific victim or perpetrator.

Surface-Enhanced Raman Spectroscopy (SERS): Evaluates how laser light reflects off a hair strand to generate unique molecular vibrations. 
Platforms like the DyeSPY machine-learning tool allow examiners to identify specific oxidative or nonoxidative dyes and predict the perceptual color of degraded hair, even if the color has faded or washed out.

Hair color can be categorized by how long the dye lasts—such as temporary, semi-permanent, demi-permanent, 
and permanent. It also refers to the coloring technique used (like balayage or highlights), 
and the natural or artificial shades themselves (like blonde, brown, red, black, or fantasy colors).
