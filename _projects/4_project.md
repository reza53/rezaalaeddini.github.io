Gait analysis is a vital tool in forensic science because it provides a reliable means of identifying or excluding suspects when traditional physical evidence, such as facial features or fingerprints, is intentionally hidden or obscured by clothing. By analyzing distinct biomechanical movement patterns, stride lengths, and tracking anomalies caught on surveillance footage or left at crime scenes, investigators can establish a behavioral fingerprint that a perpetrator cannot easily mask. While it is not as scientifically unique as DNA, its ability to bypass facial concealment and utilize low-resolution CCTV evidence makes it an indispensable corroborative tool for narrowing down suspect pools and verifying alibis.

Kinematic Software: Programs like Kinovea or Vicon are used to manually or digitally track joint angles, limb velocities, and the specific timing of stride phases directly from video frames.

<div class="caption">
    In a notable international case, a serial arsonist and thief evaded Tokyo police for months by wearing oversized clothing and masks to hide from public surveillance cameras. Investigators finally narrowed down their suspect list by using software to plot the joint angles of the perpetrator caught on camera. The metrics revealed a severe, asymmetric hip tilt caused by an old pelvic injury, allowing police to identify and arrest the correct suspect out of thousands of potential leads.
