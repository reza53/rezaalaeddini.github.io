Pollen grains are among the most resilient biological particles found in forensic samples and can link individuals or evidence to geographic regions. 
However, reliable chemical discrimination between pollen taxa and understanding of degradation patterns under real-world conditions are limited. 
Raman spectroscopy enables molecular level differentiation based on the biochemical composition of the pollen exine (sporopollenin), proteins, lipids, and pigments. 
Environmental factors such as UV radiation, humidity, and temperature alter these components, leading to measurable spectral changes. 
Combining comparative Raman profiling with aging studies will enhance the evidentiary interpretation of pollen in forensic investigations.

<div class="caption">
    Cross-pollination involves the transfer of pollen from the flower of one plant to the stigma of a genetically different plant of the same species. 
	This method promotes high genetic diversity, helping species adapt to changing environments, but it strictly relies on external agents (vectors) to move the pollen.
</div>

Biotic and abiotic agents represent the two primary pathways through which plants accomplish cross-pollination. 
Biotic agents are living organisms that actively or passively transport pollen grains from one flower to another. 
Plants that rely on these living pollinators have evolved vibrant colors, strong fragrances, and nutritious rewards like nectar to attract specific visitors. 
Insects like bees, butterflies, and beetles serve as the most common biotic vectors, though larger animals such as birds and bats also play massive roles, especially in tropical ecosystems.
In contrast, abiotic agents are non-living physical forces that move pollen entirely by chance. 
Because these environmental forces are random, plants relying on abiotic agents do not waste energy producing bright petals, sweet scents, or nectar. 
Wind is the most widespread abiotic agent, carrying massive amounts of lightweight, powdery pollen across landscapes to fertilize crops like corn, wheat, and various trees. 
Water serves as a much rarer abiotic agent, utilized almost exclusively by aquatic plants that release their pollen directly into currents to drift toward nearby female flowers.

Angiosperms are vascular plants that produce flowers and bear seeds enclosed within a fruit. Representing over 80% of all known green plants, they are the most diverse group of land plants on Earth, including everything from oak trees and grasses to roses and agricultural crops.
Angiosperms pollinate through a highly evolved lifecycle that transitions from the transfer of pollen to double fertilization
