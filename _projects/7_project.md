In forensic Bloodstain Pattern Analysis (BPA), the morphology of a bloodstain reflects the physical forces acting on a liquid droplet at the moment of impact. Passive bloodstains formed by vertical ($90^\circ$) impacts are influenced by gravitational acceleration, surface tension, fluid viscosity, and substrate texture.

A falling liquid droplet behaves as a cohesive sphere due to **surface tension**. As the droplet accelerates downward, gravitational potential energy is converted into kinetic energy.

To find where bloodshed occurred, investigators measure the width and length of blood splatters to calculate the angle of impact using trigonometry.By stringing these angles backward through space, they find the lines of convergence that intersect at the area of origin (the spatial source).This 3D reconstruction determines the exact physical location of the victim and weapon at the precise moment of the blow.
