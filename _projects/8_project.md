Nail polish is a commonly encountered cosmetic material with potential forensic
relevance in trace evidence investigations involving physical contact, assaults,
transfer events, and questioned cosmetic residues. Nail polish is a complex polymeric coating composed of film-forming agents (e.g.,
nitrocellulose, acrylates, polyesters), pigments, solvents, plasticizers, resins, and
additives.

    Nail polishes generally fall into two main categories: traditional lacquer (dries in the air) and gel polish (cures under a UV or LED lamp). They come in various special effects, including holographic, magnetic, and color-changing formulas, as well as distinct finishes like crème, shimmer, or matte.

In a high-profile case in Jiaxing, China, police discovered the dismembered torso of an unidentified woman packed into a plastic shipping bag. The body lacked any immediate identifying features except for one detail: bright red nail polish featuring intricate, custom butterfly-shaped patterns on the thumbnails.
Forensics teams recognized the design was highly unique. Detectives mapped and visited specialized nail salons across the region, eventually locating the exact technician who painted the custom butterfly art. This identified the 23-year-old victim, broke open her background, and immediately led police to arrest her boyfriend, who confessed to the crime.
