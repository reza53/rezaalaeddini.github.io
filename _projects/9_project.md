Microbial forensics helps protect national security and public safety by identifying who made or used a biological threat, telling natural outbreaks apart from bioterror attacks, and tracking evidence using unique germ signatures.

 A Louisiana gastroenterologist was accused of attempting to murder his former lover by injecting her with a substance under the guise of a vitamin B12 shot. 
 The victim later tested positive for HIV and Hepatitis C.
 Investigators seized HIV samples from Dr. Schmidt’s clinic. 
 Scientists used phylogenetic analysis (evolutionary tree mapping) to compare the genetic sequences of the virus found in the victim against the virus from the doctor's local patient samples and the wider local population. 
 The analysis proved that the victim’s HIV sequence was nearly identical to a specific sample taken from one of Schmidt's patients, proving intentional transmission. 
 Schmidt was convicted of attempted second-degree murder

While DNA sequencing is the modern gold standard, microbial forensics heavily relies on non-DNA methods. 
These techniques provide crucial contextual clues, such as how a pathogen was grown, its physical chemical structure, or how its host reacted to it.
